GCC Market Overview

Import Dependency

The GCC relies on imports for 80-90% of feed ingredients — corn, soybean meal, and vitamins primarily sourced from Brazil, the Black Sea region, and the United States. Mycotoxin risk is inherent in these supply chains.

Industrial Scaling

Vision 2030 (Saudi Arabia) and similar national food security strategies across the Gulf are driving rapid investment in industrial-scale poultry and livestock production — increasing demand for consistent, high-quality feed additives.

Climate Challenges

Extreme summer temperatures (up to 50°C) and coastal humidity create severe storage and feed quality challenges. Feed oxidation, mold growth, and vitamin degradation are year-round operational concerns.

Regulatory Standards

GCC states align with EU-equivalent feed safety standards. Saudi SFDA, UAE Ministry of Climate Change, and Qatar's Ministry of Municipality set strict tolerances for additives, contaminants, and labeling.

Key Challenges Across the GCC

🦠

Mycotoxin Risk

Imported corn and soy frequently exceed safe mycotoxin thresholds. Multi-mycotoxin strategies are essential at mill intake.

🌡️

Heat Stress

45°C+ summers damage gut integrity, increase FCR, and reduce feed intake in poultry operations across the Gulf.

🧪

Feed Oxidation

High temperatures accelerate fat oxidation and vitamin degradation in stored feed and premixes — reducing nutritional value before it reaches the animal.

📦

Storage Stability

Humid coastal conditions and long distribution chains demand feed ingredients and additives that maintain efficacy across extended shelf life.

  • Extreme heat stress: Year-round thermal pressure in central and eastern regions demands specialized nutritional support for intake and gut function.

  • Water scarcity: Feed efficiency per unit of animal product is a central economic and environmental priority across all species.
