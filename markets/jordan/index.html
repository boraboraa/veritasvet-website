Jordan Market

1. Market Overview

Jordan's animal production sector serves as a key hub for the Levant region, with poultry representing the largest segment of the country's feed and livestock industry. The country produces approximately 500 million day-old chicks annually, with a significant proportion supplied to neighboring Iraq and Syria through cross-border trade channels. Jordan's feed industry operates under pressure from water scarcity, limited arable land, and a heavy dependence on imported feed ingredients — making feed efficiency, ingredient quality, and anti-mycotoxin programs commercial priorities for feed mills and producers alike.

2. Key Challenges

  • Water scarcity: Jordan is among the world's most water-scarce countries. Feed operations must maximize nutrient utilization per unit of water, placing premium value on feed conversion efficiency and digestive nutrient absorption.
  • Import dependency for feed ingredients: Corn, soybean meal, and mineral premixes are predominantly imported — introducing variability in mycotoxin profiles and nutritional composition that requires systematic risk management at the mill level.
  • Cross-border market dynamics: Jordan's role as a supply corridor for Iraq and Syria means that feed quality standards must meet the requirements of multiple end-markets with differing regulatory frameworks.
  • Heat stress in summer: Temperatures regularly exceed 35–40°C during Jordan's summer months, impacting gut health, feed intake, and laying hen productivity across both smallholder and commercial operations.
  • Premix quality consistency: Dairy and small ruminant operations in the highlands require consistent micronutrient delivery, particularly for vitamin E, selenium, and trace minerals that support metabolic function under thermal and nutritional stress.
